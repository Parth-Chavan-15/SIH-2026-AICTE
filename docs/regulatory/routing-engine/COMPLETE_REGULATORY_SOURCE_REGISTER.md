### DOC-03: AICTE Act, 1987
- **Exact Title:** The All India Council for Technical Education Act, 1987 (Act No. 52 of 1987)
- **Exact Filename:** `AICTE Act, 1987.pdf`
- **Issuing Authority:** Parliament of India
- **Document Date:** 23 December 1987 (Came into force on 28 March 1988)
- **Applicability:** Authoritative primary parent statute
- **Page Count:** 15 physical PDF pages
- **Key Sections:**
  - *Section 2(h):* Definition of "technical education" (degree, diploma and certificate in engineering, technology, architecture, town planning, management, pharmacy, applied arts and crafts).
  - *Section 2(i):* Definition of "technical institution".
  - *Section 10(k):* Mandatory duty of Council to grant approval for starting new technical institutions and for introduction of new courses or programmes.
  - *Section 10(o):* Power to provide guidelines for admission of students.
  - *Section 10(p):* Power to inspect or cause to be inspected any technical institution.
  - *Section 11:* Inspection of technical institutions by Council committees.
  - *Section 22:* Power of Central Government to make rules.
  - *Section 23:* Power of Council to make regulations.

### DOC-08: Supreme Court Judgment (3 November 2017)
- **Exact Title:** Supreme Court of India: Orissa Lift Irrigation Corporation Ltd. v. Rabi Sankar Patro & Others (Civil Appeal Nos. 17869-17870 of 2017)
- **Exact Filename:** `Orissa Lift Irrigation Corporation Ltd. v. Rabi Sankar Patro & Others — 3 November 2017.pdf`
- **Issuing Authority:** Supreme Court of India (Coram: Adarsh Kumar Goel & Uday Umesh Lalit, JJ.)
- **Document Date:** 3 November 2017
- **Applicability:** Authoritative judicial precedent on technical degrees via distance mode
- **Page Count:** 116 physical PDF pages
- **Key Holdings (Paragraph 53):**
  - Direction I: 1994 AICTE Regulations apply to Deemed to be Universities; Deemed Universities are NOT justified in introducing new courses in technical education without AICTE approval.
  - Direction II & III: Ex-post-facto approvals granted by UGC set aside; degrees in Engineering awarded through distance education suspended.
  - Direction IV–VI: Modalities for test conducted by AICTE for 2001–2005 batch students.
  - Direction VII: Students admitted after 2005 have their distance engineering degrees cancelled and recalled.
  - Direction XI: Deemed Universities restrained from conducting distance education courses from AY 2018–19 onwards without specific statutory approval and physical inspection of centers.

### DOC-09: Supreme Court Clarification Order (22 January 2018)
- **Exact Title:** Supreme Court of India: Order in M.A. No. 38 of 2018 in Civil Appeal No. 17907 of 2017 (Orissa Lift Irrigation Clarification)
- **Exact Filename:** `Official Supreme Court order — 22 January 2018.pdf`
- **Issuing Authority:** Supreme Court of India (Coram: Adarsh Kumar Goel & Uday Umesh Lalit, JJ.)
- **Document Date:** 22 January 2018
- **Applicability:** Specific judicial clarification of 3 Nov 2017 judgment
- **Page Count:** 24 physical PDF pages
- **Key Holdings:**
  - Paragraph 3 (p. 21): Expressly clarifies that **validity of courses leading to DIPLOMAS was NOT the subject matter of the judgment**.
  - Paragraph 4: Reaffirms that degrees (graduate and post-graduate) in Engineering through distance mode were firmly governed by the judgment.
  - Paragraph 7 (pp. 22–23): Provides a **one-time relaxation** for candidates enrolled during 2001–2005 to retain their degrees until results of AICTE examination or 31 July 2018.
  - Regulatory Scope: Must not be treated as a permanent approval-routing rule or general waiver for technical education.
